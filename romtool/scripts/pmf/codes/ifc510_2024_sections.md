# IFC 2024 Section 510, verbatim extracts (read from codes.iccsafe.org, 2026-11-26)

Source (public): codes.iccsafe.org 2024 IFC chapter 5, SECTION 510 EMERGENCY RESPONDER COMMUNICATIONS ENHANCEMENT SYSTEMS (content id 31409601). The text below was read in the logged pane. The 2024 IFC now references NFPA 1225-2022.

## 510.1 ERCES in new buildings
Approved in-building ERCES for emergency responders shall be provided in all new buildings, based on the existing coverage levels of the public safety communications systems utilized by the jurisdiction, measured at the exterior of the building; the type is determined by the fire code official and the frequency license holder(s). Exceptions: (1) a wired communications system per 907.2.13.2 where approved, (2) coverage not needed per the fire code official, (3) an automatically activated system where accepted, (4) one-story buildings not exceeding 12,000 square feet (1115 m2) with no below-ground area(s).

## 510.2 Existing buildings
Existing buildings per Chapter 11.

## 510.3 Permits
Construction permit for the installation of or modification to ERCES (105.6.5); maintenance per the code is not a modification; the operational permit where required.

## 510.4 Technical requirements
Equipment required to provide the in-building ERCES shall be listed in accordance with UL 2524.

## 510.4.1 Signal strength
Acceptable where signal strength measurements in 95 percent of all areas and 99 percent of areas designated as critical areas by the fire code official on each floor meet 510.4.1.1 through 510.4.1.3.

### 510.4.1.1 Minimum signal strength into the building
The minimum downlink signal strength shall provide usable voice communications; not less than Delivered Audio Quality (DAQ) of 3.0 using narrowband analog, digital or wideband LTE signals, or an equivalent BER, or SINR applicable to the technology. (No dBm figure in the 2024 text.)

### 510.4.1.2 Minimum signal strength out of the building
The minimum uplink signal strength shall provide not less than DAQ 3.0 (same technologies).

### 510.4.1.3 System performance
Signal strength sufficient for the applications the public safety agency uses, per 510.4.2.2.

## 510.4.2 System design
Designed per 510.4.2.1 through 510.4.2.8 and NFPA 1225. 510.4.2.2: the fire code official maintains the technical criteria document (frequencies, radio sites, ERP, maximum propagation delay, applications). 510.4.2.8: near-far effect, dynamic range designed to minimize strong-signal AGC impact on weak-signal uplink.

## 510.4.2.3 Standby power
Dedicated standby batteries, or 2-hour standby batteries and connection to the facility generator power system per Section 1203. The standby power supply shall operate the system at 100-percent system capacity for not less than 12 hours.

## 510.4.2.4 Signal booster requirements
(1) All signal booster components in a NEMA Type 4 cabinet. (2) Battery systems in a NEMA 3R or higher cabinet. (3) FCC or other radio licensing authority certification, suitable for public safety use, prior to installation. (4) Donor antenna isolation from all inside antennas not less than 20 dB greater than the system gain under all operating conditions. (5) Active RF-emitting devices with built-in oscillation detection and control circuitry; supervisory signal on detection; shutdown permitted when uncorrectable. (6) Amplification systems coordinated and approved by the fire code official and the frequency license holder(s).

## 510.4.2.5 System monitoring
Monitored by a listed fire alarm control unit, or where approved an audible signal at a constantly attended on-site location. Automatic supervisory signals: (1) loss of normal AC power, (2) battery charger failure, (3) signal source malfunction, (4) failure of active RF-emitting devices, (5) low-battery capacity at 70 percent of the 12-hour operating capacity, (6) failure of critical system components, (7) the communications link to the fire alarm system, (8) oscillation of active RF-emitting devices. 510.4.2.5.1: a single supervisory input is permitted where approved.

## 510.4.2.6 Additional frequencies
Capability of modification or expansion when the FCC or other licensing authorities require frequency changes or make additional frequencies available. 510.4.2.7: as-built design documents. 510.4.2.9: booster types and uplink signal and noise levels coordinated with and approved by all affected frequency license holder(s).

## 510.5 Installation requirements
Install per NFPA 1225 and 510.5.2 through 510.5.5. 510.5.1: donor antennas permanently affixed (or an approved movable sled with the movement-prohibition sign) and per the IBC for weather protection. 510.5.2: no installation without prior coordination and approval of the fire code official and the frequency license holder. 510.5.2.1: active RF devices certified, simultaneously compatible, and written authorization from the frequency license holder(s) before initial activation. 510.5.3: personnel qualifications (FCC GROL plus in-building training or a manufacturer certificate), or demonstrated skills to the fire code official. 510.5.4: acceptance test, 95 percent per floor on the 20 equal-area grid, the 40-grid fallback (two nonadjacent failures acceptable), calibrated portable radio of the latest brand/model used by the agency, amplifier gains measured and recorded.
